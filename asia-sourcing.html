MARKET FOCUS

Factory Sourcing Asia

Asia sourcing works when supplier choice and execution controls are managed together. We support sourcing pathways across China, Vietnam, Taiwan, and India with concrete artifacts and milestone discipline.

Regional sourcing with operating structure

Factory sourcing in Asia is not one decision; it is a sequence of decisions that must remain coherent as constraints become real. Teams usually evaluate region, supplier capability, and commercial terms in separate conversations. This can create false confidence: a region appears suitable, a quote appears attractive, and then production challenges reveal capability or communication gaps. Our approach is to integrate regional sourcing decisions with execution readiness from the beginning.

We support teams evaluating China, Vietnam, Taiwan, and India by documenting assumptions, supplier fit criteria, and risk flags by region. This allows leadership to compare viable options rather than headline claims. The output is practical: shortlist memo, commercial model, and a next-phase execution plan.

What is included

  • Regional suitability review by category and complexity profile.
  • Supplier candidate mapping and fit analysis.
  • Commercial model development for MOQ/tooling/landed-cost dynamics.
  • Risk notes for schedule, quality, and communication cadence.
  • Transition plan into sampling/tooling and production gates.

Deliverables

  • Regional sourcing brief: country-level fit assumptions and constraints.
  • Factory shortlist memo: candidate profile, fit rationale, and tradeoffs.
  • Commercial baseline: cost and lead-time assumptions with risk notes.
  • Execution handoff package: next-phase milestones and required artifacts.

What success looks like

A good Asia sourcing decision is transparent and adaptable. Teams should know why a path was chosen, what assumptions underpin it, and what evidence is needed to proceed safely into sampling and tooling. This reduces surprises and enables better internal communication with finance, product, and operations stakeholders.

Failure modes prevented

  • Choosing geography based on anecdote instead of category fit.
  • Underestimating lead-time or MOQ implications by region.
  • Skipping structured supplier validation due to urgency.
  • Starting sampling without defined acceptance framework.
