What is MOQ, and how should you negotiate it?

MOQ (minimum order quantity) is the smallest batch a supplier is willing to produce under a specific cost structure. For first-time teams, MOQ can block cash flow and delay launch if not handled strategically.

When this matters

Use this guide when comparing suppliers with dramatically different MOQs and deciding whether to prioritize lower unit cost or lower inventory risk.

Decision framework

  1. Estimate realistic sales velocity for first 90 days.
  2. Calculate total landed cost at multiple MOQ tiers.
  3. Evaluate inventory carrying risk and cash cycle.
  4. Ask for phased MOQ with reorder commitment.

MOQ strategy table

StageRecommended MOQ postureTradeoff
Prototype validationLowest possible MOQ, higher unit cost acceptedProtect cash, slower margin
Pilot launchMid-tier MOQ with reorder optionBalance risk and margin
ScalingHigher MOQ with firm forecastBetter pricing, higher inventory risk

Common mistakes

  • Optimizing for unit cost while ignoring unsold inventory risk.
  • Accepting MOQ commitments without reorder confidence.
  • Treating MOQ as fixed when terms could be phased.

What to ask a factory

  • Can MOQ be split by color or variant?
  • Can we run staged deliveries on one PO?
  • What volume unlocks the next pricing tier?
