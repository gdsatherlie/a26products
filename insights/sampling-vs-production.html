Sampling vs production: why approved samples often fail in real runs.

Sampling proves possibility; production proves repeatability. Confusing these two is one of the most expensive mistakes in manufacturing. This guide explains how to move from “one good sample” to “consistent output at scale.”

In this guide

  • Key differences
  • Spec lock and golden sample
  • Sampling approval checklist
  • Change control
  • Common mistakes
  • Questions to ask factories

What changes between sample and production?

Samples are often made by senior operators with extra attention and small batch handling. Production introduces shift changes, larger lot sizes, material variability, and scheduling pressure. If process controls are weak, output drifts from sample quality.

Spec lock and golden sample

A golden sample is your physical reference. A spec lock package is your written reference. You need both. The sample shows visual and functional intent; the spec defines measurable thresholds. Without measurable thresholds, disputes become subjective.

Change control essentials

  • Every change request gets an ID and owner.
  • No implementation without impact review (cost/time/quality).
  • Approved changes update both spec lock and golden sample references.
  • Weekly review open changes until closure.

Common mistakes

  • Approving based on visual impression only.
  • Skipping pilot run before full production.
  • Allowing material substitutions without documented re-approval.

Questions to ask a factory

  • What controls ensure pilot output matches sample output?
  • How are process settings documented per line and shift?
  • How do you prevent unauthorized substitutions?
