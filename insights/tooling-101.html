Plain-English definition

Tooling is a long-lived production asset with commercial, technical, and governance implications. The most expensive tooling mistakes are usually contractual and procedural, not purely technical.

Tooling is a long-lived production asset with commercial, technical, and governance implications. The most expensive tooling mistakes are usually contractual and procedural, not purely technical. In operator practice, this should be reviewed at least weekly with a named owner and clear closure criteria, otherwise decisions degrade into assumptions and timeline risk accumulates.

Step-by-step tooling governance

Define ownership and access rights, specify revision boundaries, set payment milestones linked to evidence, run tryouts with acceptance criteria, and document maintenance responsibilities.

What it costs / what drives cost

Tooling cost drivers include part complexity, cavity count, steel choice, expected lifecycle, and revision likelihood. Change orders become costly when revision boundaries are undefined.

FAQ

When should teams prioritize tooling 101: ownership, revisions, payment structure, and change orders?

As early as possible, before high-cost commitments and before timeline pressure reduces decision quality.

How detailed should documentation be?

Detailed enough that another operator can reconstruct what was decided, by whom, and why.

Can this be done with an existing supplier?

Yes. Most improvements are operating-system improvements, not supplier replacements.

What is the first practical next step?

Create a single-page decision checklist and run it in your next supplier status review.
