Tooling 101: avoid expensive misunderstandings before you pay deposits.

Tooling decisions determine your long-term cost structure and flexibility. A weak tooling agreement can lock you into poor terms even if the unit quote looks attractive. This guide explains the terms you must define before tooling starts.

In this guide

  • Tooling basics
  • Payment structure
  • Quote red flags
  • Common mistakes
  • Questions to ask factories

Tooling basics in plain English

Tooling includes molds, fixtures, jigs, and process-specific hardware needed to manufacture repeatable output. It is usually a one-time cost but often includes iterative debugging and revision work. Teams should separate one-time tooling cost from recurring unit price to avoid confusion.

Tooling Quote Red Flags

  • No ownership clause for tooling assets.
  • No defined number of included revision rounds.
  • No acceptance criteria for tooling completion.
  • No maintenance terms (cost/frequency/responsibility).
  • Ambiguous transfer rights if supplier changes.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming tooling ownership is automatic.
  • Starting tooling before design stability is adequate.
  • Ignoring maintenance requirements until output degrades.

Questions to ask a factory

  • Who legally owns the tooling and where is it stored?
  • How many debug loops are included in quoted cost?
  • What qualifies as tooling acceptance?
  • How do you handle tooling transfer if needed?
