Tooling 101

Tooling decisions often drive quality and lead time outcomes.

In this guide

  • What this means
  • Checklist/table
  • Common mistakes
  • How engagements run

What this means

Tooling is the equipment setup used to produce your part or product repeatedly. Early tooling choices affect tolerance, repeatability, and maintenance risk.

Teams new to manufacturing should treat tooling as a controlled investment, not a one-time purchase decision.

Common mistakes

  • Starting tooling before spec lock.
  • Ignoring tolerance implications.
  • Missing maintenance and lifecycle expectations.
  • No change-control process after tooling starts.

How engagements run

Use a weekly update with milestones, risks, decisions needed, and next actions. Keep a decision log and escalate if timelines, tolerances, or quality thresholds are missed. This mirrors our homepage cadence structure.
