Tooling 101: the details that decide your real costs.

Tooling is where many first-time teams lose budget control. The quote appears simple, but terms around ownership, maintenance, revision cycles, and transfer rights can change the true cost dramatically.

When this matters

Read this before paying any tooling deposit. Once tooling begins, leverage drops and change requests become expensive.

Practical guide

  1. Separate one-time tooling from recurring unit economics.
  2. Define who owns tooling and where it is stored.
  3. Set revision assumptions and included iteration count.
  4. Define acceptance criteria for tooling completion.
  5. Clarify transfer rights if supplier relationship changes.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming tooling ownership is automatic.
  • Approving tooling before design is sufficiently stable.
  • Skipping review of post-tooling sample quality criteria.

What to ask a factory

  • Who holds the tooling physically and legally?
  • How many debug rounds are included?
  • What is your timeline by tooling milestone?
  • What happens if we need supplier transfer?
