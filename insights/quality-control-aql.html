AQL quality control in plain English: how to make shipment decisions with confidence.

AQL is often misunderstood as a universal quality guarantee. It is actually a sampling decision method. Good QC combines AQL with product-specific defect definitions, clear release thresholds, and escalation rules.

In this guide

  • AQL explained
  • Defect taxonomy
  • QC acceptance cheat sheet
  • QC plan essentials
  • Common mistakes

AQL explained

AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) sets the maximum defect rate considered acceptable for an inspection lot under a defined sampling plan. It does not replace engineering standards. It helps structure pass/fail decisions when inspecting a subset of units.

Defect taxonomy

  • Critical: safety, legal, or severe functional risk.
  • Major: significant functional or customer-impact issue.
  • Minor: cosmetic deviation with low functional impact.

QC Acceptance Criteria Cheat Sheet

Defect classRelease thresholdAction
Critical0 allowedImmediate hold + containment
MajorLow tolerance by AQL planCorrective action + recheck
MinorHigher toleranceTrend monitoring and follow-up

What to include in a QC plan

  • Inspection scope and timing (in-line, pre-shipment, etc.).
  • Sampling method and lot sizing assumptions.
  • Defect library with photos/examples.
  • Escalation path and release decision authority.
  • Corrective action closure requirements.

Common mistakes

  • Using generic defect definitions not tailored to product function.
  • Changing acceptance thresholds after seeing inspection outcomes.
  • Shipping with unresolved major defects due schedule pressure.

Questions to ask factory and inspectors

  • How are defect definitions trained across inspectors?
  • What triggers rework versus lot hold?
  • How are recurring defects tracked over time?

  • Release thresholds must be set before inspection.
