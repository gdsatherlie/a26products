Quality control and AQL, explained in plain English.

AQL is a sampling method, not a quality strategy by itself. Teams get into trouble when they treat AQL as the only decision rule and ignore product-critical defects or process trends.

When this matters

Use this guide before pre-shipment inspections are booked. If criteria are defined too late, inspections become negotiation rather than control.

How to set useful QC criteria

  1. Define critical/major/minor defects for your product.
  2. Map defects to customer impact, not just visual appearance.
  3. Set sampling plan and AQL levels by defect class.
  4. Predefine containment actions and release decisions.

QC Acceptance Criteria Cheat Sheet (linkable asset)

Defect classTypical impactSuggested rule
CriticalSafety or legal riskZero tolerance; automatic hold

MinorCosmetic varianceHigher AQL allowed with trend watch

Common mistakes

  • No written defect library with examples.
  • Changing AQL targets after results are known.
  • Shipping with open major defects due schedule pressure.

What to ask a factory or inspector

  • How are defect classes trained across inspectors?
  • How are repeat defects tracked across batches?
  • What containment is required for major defects?
