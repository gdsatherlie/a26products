BEGINNER PILLAR

How to manufacture a product: a practical path from idea to shipment.

If this is your first time manufacturing, the process can look chaotic because each factory explains things differently. This guide gives one standard path you can use regardless of country or product category. It focuses on execution: who decides what, when to lock details, and how to avoid expensive late-stage surprises.

When this matters most

This guide matters when you have a prototype or concept and need a real plan. It also matters when your team is hearing conflicting advice from brokers, factories, and logistics partners. The goal is not theory; the goal is to move from “we should start” to “we know what happens next and why.”

Step-by-step framework

  1. Define scope: product requirements, target cost, and launch window.
  2. Build supplier longlist: shortlist factories by capability, not only price.
  3. Run sample cycles: test critical features and document all defects.
  4. Lock specification: freeze approved version before production order.
  5. Manage production: run weekly milestones, risk reviews, and escalation.
  6. QC and release: apply acceptance criteria and release gates.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a supplier before validating process maturity.
  • Using sample approval as a substitute for written criteria.
  • Changing specs during production without a change log.
  • Waiting until pre-shipment inspection to define quality thresholds.

What to ask a factory

  • Which process steps are in-house versus outsourced?
  • What tolerance ranges are realistic for this design?
  • What does your sample-to-production transfer process look like?
  • What metrics do you track weekly during production?
  • How do you handle defect containment before shipment?
