How to manufacture a product

Most first production runs fail from procedural gaps, not one dramatic mistake. This guide shows how to run sourcing and production with clear controls from day one.

Who this is for

Founders and operators launching a first run, moving from prototype into production, or inheriting a factory relationship without clear controls.

Key takeaways

  • Supplier selection is a risk decision, not a quote comparison.
  • Sample approval without spec lock creates expensive drift.
  • Weekly decision cadence is what prevents late surprises.
  • Release criteria must be set before inspection starts.

Execution roadmap

1) Define the commercial and technical boundary

Write one page that states target cost, MOQ, acceptable lead-time range, non-negotiable quality criteria, and likely compromise points.

2) Validate supplier capability before deposits

Confirm process fit, category experience, and communication discipline. A fast response is not proof of execution reliability.

3) Control sample revisions

Each sample iteration should record what changed, who approved it, and what remains unresolved.

4) Lock production-critical details

Before production PO, lock BOM assumptions, packaging details, inspection scope, and escalation thresholds.

5) Track production with owner-level cadence

Run weekly milestones, blockers, and decision logs. If no owner and date are attached, the issue is not controlled.

6) Release only against documented criteria

Define pass/hold/rework logic in advance so shipment release is not a negotiation under schedule pressure.

Practical checklist (copy/paste)

[ ] Requirements brief approved internally
[ ] Target cost/MOQ/timeline ranges documented
[ ] Supplier validation checks completed
[ ] Sample revision log up to date
[ ] Change control method agreed
[ ] Milestone owners assigned
[ ] QC scope + release criteria approved
[ ] Shipment release decision owner assigned

Common mistakes

  • Choosing suppliers based only on initial unit price.
  • Treating sample approval as production readiness.
  • Discovering quality criteria at final inspection stage.
  • Letting change orders happen through chat without a log.

What we do in practice

We build one operating thread from sourcing through release: scorecard, revision log, milestone tracker, and shipment criteria. This gives small teams institutional-level control without building a full internal sourcing department.

FAQ

Do I need final CAD before talking to factories?

No, but you need enough clarity to define tolerances, materials, and acceptance priorities.

How many suppliers should I evaluate?

Typically 3–5 serious candidates are enough to compare capability and risk profile.

When should I pay tooling deposits?

After ownership terms, revision process, and trial expectations are documented.

Can this process work with a current supplier?

Yes. Most gains come from better controls, not switching factories.
