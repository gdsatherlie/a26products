SERVICE CLUSTER

Sampling & Tooling Management

Sampling should narrow uncertainty, not create endless loops. Our sampling and tooling governance process creates objective acceptance criteria and a controlled path to spec lock.

What this cluster covers

Sampling is where many programs lose control. Teams often run multiple sample rounds without defining what constitutes acceptance, who owns a decision, or how changes are governed. Tooling then proceeds on assumptions that were never fully resolved. The result is predictable: expensive resets, delayed production starts, and avoidable quality variance. Our approach is designed to prevent that by introducing disciplined sample objectives and decision control.

Every sample round starts with a specific objective and a clear acceptance checklist. Review findings are documented in a tracker that includes owner, due date, and closure state. Tooling readiness is monitored in parallel so sample decisions and tooling decisions remain synchronized. Once criteria are met, the program moves to formal spec lock and change control.

Included operating components

  • Sample-round objective definition and review cadence.
  • Acceptance criteria by dimension, finish, performance, and assembly behavior.
  • Tooling readiness checkpoints tied to sample findings.
  • Change request logging with impact notation.
  • Spec lock package and controlled revision rules.

Deliverables and artifacts

  • Sampling tracker: round status, findings, actions, owner, closure date.
  • Acceptance criteria matrix: objective pass/fail conditions per sample phase.
  • Spec lock document: approved build definition with references.
  • Change control register: requested change, rationale, impact, approval.
  • Tooling checkpoint log: readiness status and risk notes before scale-up.

What success looks like

Success is not “sample approved” as a standalone statement. Success is sample approval with clear evidence, a locked spec baseline, and an agreed process for handling late changes. Teams should enter production knowing what is fixed, what can change, and who has final decision authority.

Failure modes prevented

  • Sample cycles with no closure criteria.
  • Tooling commitments before critical tolerances are resolved.
  • Untracked revision requests that create undocumented product drift.
  • Disputes over what was approved once production starts.
